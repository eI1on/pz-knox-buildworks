[b]Windows, Gables & Wall Finishes Update[/b]
[list]
[*][b]382 walls became 857, and 21 windows became 91.[/b] Nearly every family is bigger, and about 3,800 sprites the game had drawn, but nothing used, now are buildable.
[*][b]Window walls.[/b] Eight window designs for most wall families - arched, square, framed, wide, round, full height, plus a double door and a double window two squares across. Grouped by design, so one entry cycles the pieces that make up a window.
[*][b]70 windows to go in them.[/b] Church, metal, white and wooden, in every shape the frames come in. A window only fits an opening it was drawn for, so a frame of the wrong shape is refused rather than letting you build something that looks wrong.
[*][b]30-degree gables for every family.[/b] The last update added shallow roofs but nothing to close their ends. Every family that had steep gables now has shallow ones too.
[*][b]Bathroom tiles: one tiling became thirteen.[/b] Wall, corner, pillar, window frame and door frame in every tiling on the sheet, and nine of them have window walls as well.
[*][b]Blended paints.[/b] A finish can call for two cans: Classic Brick gains a tan and a forest green, Cinderblock a dark grey. The materials panel shows each can and what it costs.
[*][b]Old / Weathered Brick and Poor Brick are variants, not paints.[/b] Both were hiding in Classic Brick's colour list. They are their own look now, on every piece, and the poor brick takes no finish at all - it is an unfinished wall.
[*][b]Painting a wall now paints its gables.[/b] Three families changed the wall and left the gable in the old colour. Corner walls took the flat wall's sprite when painted; they take a corner now.
[*][b]The vanilla window frames sit with their walls now.[/b] Brick, log, metal, stone and wooden window frames were filed under a Window Frames list of their own. Each family has one entry again: the poor and shoddy grades cycle under it the way the walls do, and the old vanilla look is kept as a variant rather than a second entry.
[*][b]The Buildable Editor is a debug tool in singleplayer.[/b] It was on the dock for every solo game, though it rewrites the rules the whole catalogue is built from. It now appears only when the game is launched in debug mode. Multiplayer is unchanged - admins only.
[*][b]Stair railings.[/b] Five of them - wood and metal, metal, white metal, wooden and white wooden - under Fencing > Stair Railings. Each is a full flight in one build: three squares of railing along the stairs and two more on the level above, placed in one go and running in either direction.
[*][b]Built doors can be locked again.[/b] The key id on the doorknob you put into a door was thrown away, so every door and gate built through Buildworks came out keyed to nothing and no key would ever fit it. The doorknob now hands its key to the finished door, garage doors included, and both halves of a double door share the one key. Doors built before this update are unaffected.
[*][b]Overhead cabinets hang where you would expect them to.[/b] They refused any square that already held a counter or a cabinet, which is exactly where a kitchen puts them, and they would not go on a door or window frame either. Both now work - the cabinet hangs above whatever stands under it. Corner cabinets took support only from one of the two walls they touch, so whether they went up depended on which wall you had built; either wall carries them now.
[*][b]The trailer kitchen, finished.[/b] The floating trailer counter only had two of its four sides, so it turned into a doorless box half the time - the missing two are in. Added the counter corner and the floating counter corner to go with them, and the built-in trailer fridge: a fridge with a small freezer recessed into a wooden cupboard, costing a small fridge worth of parts plus planks for the cabinet around it.
[*][b]A new floor now replaces the old one.[/b] Laying a floor over another left the square still drawing the old one until the chunk reloaded, so the floor you had just paid for looked like it had not been built - and the grass or overlay underneath was never cleared. Floors were the one build that skipped the redraw every other build does, and on a server nothing redrew them at all.
[*][b]Three floors were the same floor twice.[/b] Cinnamon and Chestnut each had a separate West and North entry, and the "White" and "Grey" Plank floors are one floor in two rotations. Each is a single entry now that you rotate, like every other floor.
[/list]

[b]Durability, Mannequins & Planning Traffic Update[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Mannequins are real mannequins:[/b] Built mannequins were plain tile objects, so they had no storage, showed no capacity and could not be looted or dressed. All twelve are now built as the base game's own mannequin: clothes go on them exactly as on one found in a mall, and the build menu lists their capacity. A mannequin you build arrives undressed rather than in a random outfit, faces any of four directions, and previews the posed body while placing instead of a flat marker. The two Mannequin Stands are unaffected. Mannequins built before this update keep their old form.
[*][b]Planning drag-fill traffic:[/b] A drag-fill sent one command per tile and the server echoed every one to each viewer, so a single large drag could fire several hundred commands in seconds. Drags are now batched - a 24x24 fill goes from around 576 commands to around 18 - and the blueprint is written to disk once per batch. The server still validates every tile individually on the same rules, passes on only what it accepts, and corrects the dragging player in one message if anything is refused.
[*][b]Planning mode switch is authoritative:[/b] With EnablePlanningMode off, the server refuses every blueprint command outright instead of relying on the client to hide the buttons. Erasing a placement, removing a room and deleting a blueprint stay allowed, so turning the option off can never strand a player with plans they cannot clear.
[*][b]Construction durability, reworked:[/b] Almost every Buildworks structure finished at a flat 100 health with no skill scaling, so a high fence was no sturdier than a poster while entity-backed vanilla walls kept their 450-800. Health is now derived from what the piece is (wall, fence, railing, frame, door, floor, stairs, container, workstation, furniture, decoration, overlay), scaled by its material and by the skill its recipe demands. Metal, brick, stone and concrete beat wood; fabric and glass fall below it; masonry gains far more per level than carpentry. The formula is unchanged and anything a definition or vanilla script states explicitly still wins, so no vanilla buildable's health moved.
[*][b]Single-tile high fences:[/b] Every two-tile high fence - chainlink and razor wire, decorative wooden, wooden, wrought iron, wired and wired razor - can now be built one tile at a time, offered alongside the full panel for half the materials and half the build time. Runs no longer have to come out an even number of tiles long.
[*][b]More railings:[/b] Added the wood-and-metal and plain metal railings with their corner and post pieces, plus the two remaining wooden variants, under Fencing > Railing.
[*][b]Workshop fixtures now actually appear:[/b] The Wooden Pegboard and Wood-Top Workbench were never listed in the add-on manifest, so the game never loaded them. They are in Furniture > Workstations now.
[*][b]Propane torches drained past empty:[/b] A build that needed more fuel than the first blowtorch had left kept draining that same torch instead of moving to the next one, pushing it below empty while a full one sat beside it. Exhausted drainables are now skipped, so fuel is spent across your torches exactly as the catalogue counted it.
[*][b]Double door frames replacing their wall frame:[/b] Building a double door frame onto an existing wall frame left the old frame standing, so it had to be dismantled by hand. Fixes all twelve black, white, grey, chestnut, brown industrial and chestnut industrial double door frames, and any other passable wall piece built over a frame.
[*][b]Rusty Mesh, Rusty Mesh Grate and Steel Bars build as real floors:[/b] These three were the only Floors entries still marked as objects, so they could only be laid on top of an existing floor instead of extending one. They now place like every other floor, needing adjacent support rather than a floor beneath them.
[*][b]Flooring, filled out:[/b] Floors go from 78 to 148 from art that was sitting unused on six tilesheets: sixteen patterned carpets (persian, kilim, floral, geometric, striped, ribbed, medallion), sixteen more woven carpet colours, eleven exterior stone and paver patterns plus a speckled terrazzo, nine smooth interior tiles including three with a coloured inlay, a new Tile Square set of eight, two painted plank floors and eight shag rugs. Every piece takes the recipe its subcategory already uses.
[*][b]Roofing, rebuilt from the ground up:[/b] Roofing goes from 205 pieces to 1033 and every roof sheet in the game is now covered. They all turned out to carry the same ten colours at three pitches, so they are filed one subcategory per colour, holding that colour at every pitch it exists in. Sage green and white are new families. All ten 30-degree sheets arrive with nine piece groups each - slope, hip end, hip ridge, valley, pyramid, half slope, ridge, eave, flat hip - running six levels that sit side by side on one Z level to extend a roof. The leftover shingle rows are in as well. Roof trim is new: fascia, corners, peaks and downpipes at both pitches, each run and its opposite one buildable with two rotations. Every piece takes its colour family's own recipe, so glass roofs weld glass panels and clay-tiled families ask for shingles.
[*][b]Patching a vanilla buildable from an add-on:[/b] Definition files can now carry a [i]patches[/i] block that re-files an already-registered buildable - category, subcategory, name, material and group. Previously this needed a hand-edited overrides file in your own Lua folder, which an add-on could not ship. First use: with Vanilla Expanded enabled, the vanilla Big Wire Fence becomes Fence Chain (Half B) inside the same Fencing > High group as the Fence Chain corner and post.
[*][b]Translations swept end to end:[/b] One roofing tooltip key had never existed, so 42 trim pieces fell back to raw text. Every key the definitions ask for now resolves, all eight languages carry identical key sets across all six translation files, and 39 keys left behind by renamed or merged buildables were removed.
[*][b]Name and spelling fixes:[/b] Victorian 1 Victorian 1 Rug, Fascia Accent Top Corner Corner and Stained Wood Wood Panneling each had a word repeated, with sixteen more in Romanian. Panelling was misspelled as Panneling in all 32 places it appeared, and ten roofing names carried a double space, as did the blueprint summary and tile coordinate lines in Spanish, Russian and Chinese. Buildable ids are unchanged, so existing blueprints keep working.
[*][b]Definition fixes:[/b] The Classic Brick Wall declared no material and was rated as timber rather than masonry. The Brown Sliding Glass Door Side Wall shipped an empty placement while its white twin was correct, so it placed as a free-standing object instead of on a wall edge. The indoor lighting set spelled its material "Electric" against the catalogue's "Electronics", splitting the material filter in two. A dozen buildables could fall back to raw identifiers such as "PaintSign_ArrowDown", the fireplace jambs spelled "Gray" against the catalogue's "Grey", and Greene's Grocery Display Counter carried a stray space in its English name.
[*][b]Buildables fixes:[/b] Piano Stool sprite orientations.
[/list]

[b]Timber Framing, Working Fixtures & Wall Connections Update[/b]
[list]
[*][b]Wall-frame construction:[/b] Corrected wooden and metal frame connection sprites so joined frames use their complete V-shaped corner while offset joins use the separate gap-filling post. Frame corners no longer disappear into a post or attempt to attach an incompatible native entity configuration.
[*][b]T-junction walls and fences:[/b] Joining a wall or fence onto a run that continues past the junction no longer merges the two edges into a single corner piece. Wall frames, big wire fences and every other buildable with a corner sprite now appear as placed at a T-section instead of consuming both the new segment and part of the existing run.
[*][b]Vanilla wall and fence corners restored:[/b] Every entity-backed vanilla wall, fence, door frame and window frame treated its script's corner sprite as a joined corner piece, replacing both meeting edges with a single post. That sprite is the base game's gap filler for walls that meet diagonally, so it now fills the diagonal square as the base game does and leaves both walls standing. The wooden and metal wall frames keep their dedicated V-shaped joined corner.
[*][b]Materials carried in bags:[/b] Entity-backed recipes such as the Double Door no longer report planks, hinges or doorknobs as unavailable when they are inside a backpack. The authoritative server pass now lists every carried container instead of falling back to the main inventory alone, so dedicated servers accept the same materials the catalogue counted.
[*][b]Metal drums, rebuilt:[/b] Player-built metal drums never filled with water. They referenced the base game's Rain Collector entity, but that entity's SpriteConfig does not list the drum sprites, so the engine skipped the whole attachment and the drum was left without the FluidContainer component that B42 uses for rain. Buildworks now creates and configures that component itself. A single barrel also serves as either a rain collector or a burn barrel: a context-menu option switches between the two and swaps the object for the matching kind, so each mode shows only the options that belong to it instead of stacking the fluid and fireplace menus on top of each other. The Buildworks options sit directly under the drum's own context entry, and because the base game has no way at all to take fireplace fuel back out, a Dump Out Fuel option is provided. Switching refuses only while the drum still holds water or a live fire. A collecting drum shows its water sprite as it fills, and a burning one picks up the flame overlay the base game already reserves for metal drums. Both drums moved from Survival to Outdoors > Water Collectors on their correct grey and orange sprites instead of sharing one, and the welding recipe is normalised to a single sensible cost. Added the sealed metal drum as well: it holds no water and cannot be lit, but it is placed as a moveable so it can be picked back up and spent on a Charcoal Burner.
[*][b]All four generators:[/b] Only the red generator could be built. The old, blue and yellow units are now buildable too. They are not reskins: the base game reads sound radius and wear rate off the item behind the generator, so the yellow unit is the quietest and slowest to wear out, the red one is close behind, the blue frame is the lightest to carry but wears out fastest, and the old unit is the loudest and nearly as short lived. Their recipes are priced against exactly that, and all four are now a serious build: welding alongside electrics, engine parts for the better three, and up to 16 wire, 32 electronics scrap and 8 sheet metal for the yellow one.
[*][b]Church set:[/b] Added a Furniture > Church group with the two-tile altar, the lectern in all four facings, the organ stool and a full three-tile pipe organ, plus the three evangelical banners under Decorations > Banners. The organ is the first Buildworks object to stand two levels tall and is priced as a master carpenter's project.
[*][b]Brazier and log railing:[/b] Added the stone brazier under Survival > Fireplace, which takes fuel and lights like any other fireplace and picks up the base game's own fuelled and lit overlays. Added the log railing under Fencing > Railing: sections that meet join into the V-shaped corner piece on a single tile, and ends that meet offset get the free-standing pole dropped into the gap.
[*][b]Log Cabin Door Frame:[/b] The log cabin door frame is now a real door frame. It no longer registers as a solid wall edge, so players can walk through the doorway and hang a door in it as with every other door frame.
[*][b]New Structural category:[/b] Added 31 timber framing buildables covering the whole unused carpentry_03 tile sheet: plain, short, braced, reverse-braced and double-braced posts; beams, stubs, blocks and beam corners at both floor and ceiling height; small, medium, large and gable trusses; small, medium, tall and trestle frames; four heights of railing; and two cradle brackets. Posts, beams and their corners carry all four rotations, trusses, frames and railings carry both, and beams stay walk-through so they can run across a floor or overhead without blocking anyone. Framing pieces can be combined freely on a single tile, so a post, its brace and the beam it carries all share one square, in Planning Mode as well as direct build. A post no longer seals its tile against movement either. Translated into every supported language.
[*][b]Entity-backed multiplayer construction:[/b] Aligned native workstation and appliance creation with B42.20's server-side entity lifecycle. Buildworks now resolves the complete footprint before consuming materials, keeps an existing frame or wall until the replacement object is ready, and reports invalid resolved faces instead of silently consuming the recipe. This covers cases where objects such as the Pottery Bench appeared to finish without leaving a usable object.
[*][b]Safe wall and frame replacement:[/b] Building onto a planned or existing frame can no longer remove the frame first and then fail with an empty result. Solid player-built walls remain directly replaceable, while multi-tile replacement preserves the original object's position in the square.
[*][b]Garage-door stability:[/b] Garage-door sprites use the dedicated IsoDoor path so their open sprite is resolved from the native GarageDoor properties. This prevents null-sprite crashes in door toggling and the B42 lighting thread while preserving correct inside/outside orientation.
[*][b]Lighter well streaming:[/b] Well detection now checks the vanilla well sprite and hasModData before reading object mod data, avoiding empty mod-data table allocations on every unrelated object loaded with a square.
[*][b]Workshop fixtures:[/b] Added the two-tile Wooden Pegboard and Wood-Top Workbench with all four rotations, native wall/table properties, balanced carpentry and welding recipes, and translations in every supported language.
[*][b]Cleaner sprite diagnostics:[/b] Expected sprite sharing can now be declared by the definition that intentionally reuses it. Material and finish variants inside one buildable no longer generate duplicate warnings, each real cross-buildable conflict is reported only once, and moveable/prop objects no longer receive incompatible SpriteConfig components.
[*][b]Translations:[/b] All eight languages carry every name and tooltip the definitions ask for, and 1,372 keys left behind by renamed, merged or folded entries were removed.
[/list]

[b]Previous Update - UI, Accessibility & B42.20 Compatibility[/b]

[list]
[*][b]Responsive catalogue:[/b] Reworked the layout for large fonts, smaller windows and different resolutions. The catalogue now opens at a more practical size and remembers the dimensions of each display mode.
[*][b]Compact mode:[/b] Added a condensed icon grid, B41 Building Menu inspired subcategory navigation, grouped-buildable carousel and full material/finish options.
[*][b]Layout fixes:[/b] Fixed blocked resize handles after switching modes, incorrect scrollbar sizing, clipped or overflowing text, overlapping controls and large-font issues in the catalogue and Planning Mode.
[*][b]Buildable information:[/b] Added dimensions and storage capacities, including separate values for each available tile and container on multi-tile or multi-container objects.
[*][b]Improved tooltips:[/b] Compact-mode tooltips now show item icons, descriptions, requirements, dimensions and storage information.
[*][b]Appearance and accessibility:[/b] Added an appearance menu with multiple themes, opacity and high-contrast settings, plus checkerboard, light, dark and custom-colour buildable backgrounds.
[*][b]Detailed-list performance:[/b] Greatly reduced freezing when opening the detailed list, selecting buildables or resizing the window. Detailed rows now respect the selected preview background and wrap long text correctly.
[*][b]Translations and descriptions:[/b] Shortened and consolidated reusable English and Simplified Chinese descriptions, moved shared category and subcategory translations into the main mod.
[*][b]Military crates:[/b] Added green, gray and white military crates with 100 storage capacity. Every variant can be painted using the white directional base sprites and the game's native custom-colour system.
[*][b]B42.20 multiplayer compatibility:[/b] Fixed construction XP not being awarded correctly after completing buildables in multiplayer.
[*][b]B42.20 file compatibility:[/b] Blueprint saves and exports now use JSON-formatted .txt files. Existing .json blueprints remain readable and are migrated automatically, while buildable definition files remain in JSON format.
[*][b]General fixes:[/b] Restored and improved grouped carousels and selectors, fixed group/finish preview updates, and corrected the Double Wrought Iron Gate's erroneous door-frame requirement.
[/list]
